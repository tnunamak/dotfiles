## SUMMARY

The prior FP/Hickey-centric theory is myopic and meaningfully biased, but its language-agnostic core survives. The strongest adversarial finding is structural: code-quality history contains a famous, primary-sourced, named case AGAINST the Lisp/elegance worldview — Richard Gabriel's "Worse Is Better," which argues the pragmatic New Jersey/Unix-C school (implementation simplicity above interface, correctness, consistency, and completeness) has better survival characteristics than the MIT/Lisp "Right Thing," and explicitly maps the elegance camp to Common Lisp/CLOS/Scheme. Hickey's headline thesis ("mutable state is the number one problem in systems") is a genuine, well-attributed philosophical position — confirming Hickey as a paragon of TALKING about quality — but the verified evidence does NOT establish his code as held up as exemplary the way Gabriel's argument or the imperative-paragon practices are. The durable, paradigm-independent spine is "minimize incidental complexity / ruthless simplicity," a value the FP camp (Hickey) and the anti-FP camp (Gabriel's worse-is-better) BOTH claim — proving simplicity is the robust core while "immutability/purity" is the FP-flavored artifact to demote. Net: decenter Hickey/FP as the spine, promote the pragmatic-systems canon (Gabriel, and by the strong-but-unchallenged supporting evidence, Carmack-as-pragmatist, SQLite's verification culture, Pike's "data dominates"), and treat simplicity-and-verification — not paradigm — as the quality center.
