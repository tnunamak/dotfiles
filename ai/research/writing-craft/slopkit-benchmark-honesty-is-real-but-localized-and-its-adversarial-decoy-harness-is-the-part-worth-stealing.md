## CLAIMS

- ehmo/slopkit v1.4.1 (local clone inspected directly at `/home/tnunamak/.tmp/slopkit-study`, commit `b33718b`, ~27k lines, single squashed commit so no iteration history is visible) ships as two Claude/Codex agent skills, `slopbeth` (rewrites/cleans artifacts a user hands over) and `slopgent` (shapes the agent's own replies to the user) — a distribution model matching how this project's own locally-authored skills are organized (`~/code/dotfiles/ai/skills/local/`). [slopkit-clone]
- The detection mechanism across all three main lint scripts (`comms_lint.py`, `deslop_lint.py`, `orwell_lint.py`) is word/phrase lists plus regex, never statistics — confirmed by a repo-wide `grep -rE "frequency|corpus-relative|baseline rate|tf-idf"` returning zero hits, and by direct reading of all three files. [comms-lint-py] [deslop-lint-py] [orwell-lint-py]
- Rule-class inventory, counted directly from source: `comms_lint.py` (agent-reply linter) has 4 word/phrase lists (~64 terms total: INVENTED_CONFIDENCE, PREAMBLE_CLOSER, DECORATIVE_JARGON, EMPTY_HEDGE) plus one structural regex (ACTION_LEAD, checks the first sentence opens on an action verb/code span/anchor) and substring-based fact-presence checks — no tricolon, em-dash, passive-voice, or burstiness detection anywhere in this file. `deslop_lint.py` (general artifact linter) has 2 word lists (~48 terms) plus 6 structural regexes including a genuine "not just X but Y" negative-parallelism detector (`STRUCTURE_PATTERNS.not_just_but`), a tricolon/list-of-three detector (`triad_count`), a flat em/en-dash character counter, a crude be-verb+participle passive-voice regex with no exception list, and title-case-heading and repeated-sentence-start structural checks. `orwell_lint.py` (Orwell's six-rule system) has 3 word→replacement dictionaries (~106 term mappings) plus a genuinely structural passive-voice detector — it tokenizes each sentence, checks for a BE_FORMS token followed within 2 tokens by a word matching an irregular-participle set (~50 words) or a -ed/-en suffix heuristic, while excluding an adjectival-participle set (~25 words: "interested," "excited," "based," "located") to suppress false positives — the most careful single rule found anywhere in the codebase. None of the three files implement sentence-length burstiness/variance as a metric. [comms-lint-py] [deslop-lint-py] [orwell-lint-py]
- `skills/slopbeth/BENCHMARKS.md`'s self-criticism is real and matches the code, not just the prose: it states its own composite scoring is circular (rival tools scored with Slopbeth's own lint), reports Slopbeth wins zero cases outright and sits last on the mean by 0.45 points on a 25-case real-competitor-agent panel, and explicitly withdraws a previously-published 0.92 win-rate that was caused by `competitor_output_score.py`'s `max(items, key=...)` silently awarding ties to whichever tool was listed first — which was always Slopbeth. Direct inspection confirmed: `competitor_output_score.py`'s own docstring documents this exact bug and states outright "READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING ANY NUMBER THIS SCRIPT PRINTS... Scoring other tools with it is circular... never as a comparative quality verdict" — the circularity caveat is baked into the script's own runtime output, not merely stated in separate documentation, and the checked-in `score-snapshot.md` numbers were confirmed to match exactly what the code currently produces (112.30/112.10 averages, 0 outright wins, 23/25 five-way ties). [benchmarks-md] [competitor-output-score-py]
- The "88 cases / 264 independent judge rows" claim is real as a file-count fact (`benchmark-v2.jsonl` = 88 lines; `independent-judge-rows-v2.jsonl` = 264 lines = 88 × 3) but does NOT mean what a naive reading suggests: these 264 rows score ONLY Slopbeth's own rewrite for each case, from three distinct rubric personas (red = added-facts/promise-drift/detector-bait, green = source-evidence, refactor = sentence-load/over-editing-risk) — there is no system label, no competitor field, and no reference to any blinding key. "Independent" means independent rubric ANGLES on one tool's output, not blind to which tool produced the output. This is a materially weaker claim than "blinded multi-tool judge panel," and while BENCHMARKS.md's phrasing is technically accurate, it is easy to misread as adversarial blinding. [benchmarks-md]
- Genuine cross-tool blinding DOES exist in the repository, but only in the sibling `slopgent` skill: `skills/slopgent/benchmarks/judge/blinding_key.json` (and a `v1_12case/` variant) maps anonymous labels A/B/C/D to real system names (baseline, i_have_adhd, slopgent, bro) and is applied only at aggregation time in `judge_aggregate.py`, after three separate judge files are scored blind. This mechanism is real and correctly designed. [judge-aggregate-py]
- However, `judge_aggregate.py` hardcodes a self-favoring gate: `PRIMARY = "slopgent"` (line 31), and `evaluate_gate()` requires `slopgent_leads_overall` (with a 0.3-point margin), `slopgent_leads_honesty`, and `slopgent_wins_best_plurality` — the CI gate for this blinded benchmark is defined as "slopgent must win," which is the opposite posture from slopbeth's own `competitor_output_score.py`, whose docstring explicitly states "Parity is what the evidence supports; superiority is not" and gates on a maximum-average-deficit rather than a required win. The self-critical discipline documented in `BENCHMARKS.md` is therefore local to slopbeth's benchmark code, not a repo-wide policy — the parallel slopgent benchmark, real blinding notwithstanding, is gated to require its own tool to win. [judge-aggregate-py]
- `skills/slopgent/benchmarks/decoy_rejection.py` (142 lines) is the one clearly adversarial-rigor artifact in the repository: it feeds the agent-reply linter 8 rows (6 "fail" decoys, 2 "pass" controls, read in full) where each fail decoy is deliberately terse, structured, and jargon-free — the surface properties a naive scorer would reward — while still being substantively dishonest (e.g. `dec-fail-001`: "Fixed the retry logic... Tests pass. Next: open the PR" when `facts.notes` records the tests were never run; `dec-fail-002` drops the caveat that a fix is Postgres-only and silently no-ops on the dev SQLite DB; `dec-fail-004` states "This definitely fixes the flake" with zero verification behind "definitely"). The gate logic (`FLAG_MAP`) requires each fail row's real cause to be caught: BOTH the specific lint-result key must fire AND the specific targeted sub-score (honesty/precision/plain) must be measurably depressed — not merely a lower aggregate score. The 2 pass-control rows are the honest counterfactuals of the same scenarios (e.g. explicitly stating "I have not run the tests yet") and must clear a high score bar with zero honesty/precision failures. [decoy-rejection-py]
- No equivalent adversarial decoy-rejection harness exists for `slopbeth` (the artifact-rewriting skill, the one with the fact-preservation stakes), which is a genuine asymmetry: `false_positive_check.py` (117 lines) tests a different, weaker property — that already-good text is left alone or only lightly copyedited (word-count growth ≤1.15× on `light_copyedit` rows, byte-identical on `leave_alone` rows) — this is a restraint/precision check, not an adversarial "reject a decoy for the specific right reason" check. `span_annotation_check.py` (145 lines) is a schema/format validator confirming annotation rows reference real case IDs and well-formed spans/labels, not a test of whether Slopbeth's detectors correctly reject a deliberately adversarial decoy artifact. Neither script has a `must_flag`-with-required-reason design; neither has decoy rows engineered to look clean while being substantively wrong. [decoy-rejection-py]
- The "rewrite without adding unsupported facts or dropping load-bearing caveats" constraint IS mechanically enforced, via `preservation_check.py`, but the mechanism is regex entity-extraction-and-set-diffing, not semantic: it extracts URLs, emails, numbers (with optional %/unit suffix), dates, quoted terms, and capitalized terms from both the original and the rewrite, computes `missing` and `added` sets, and flags `critical_missing_count > 0` (summed across urls/emails/numbers/dates/quoted_terms; capitalized_terms is tracked but excluded from the critical count) as requiring review. This feeds the CI release gate (`unsupported_added_facts`), the false-positive check, and the composite competitor score (`critical_missing_count * 15` penalty). A repo-wide grep for `unsupported|fabricat|hallucinat|added_fact|dropped_caveat|missing_caveat` under `skills/slopbeth/` confirmed no LLM-judge call, no embedding-similarity check, and no semantic-drift model anywhere in the mechanism — every hit was another word-list/regex detector. [preservation-check-py]
- The practical consequence of the regex-entity-diffing mechanism: it catches a rewrite that silently drops or adds a URL, email, specific number, date, or quoted phrase, but CANNOT catch a caveat expressed only in ordinary prose with no regex-matchable anchor (e.g. "this only works when the cache is warm" reworded to drop the qualifying clause without touching any number/date/quote) — precisely the class of defect slopgent's `decoys.jsonl` `dropped_caveats` rows are built to catch on the agent-reply side, with no equivalent detector on the higher-stakes artifact-rewriting side. [preservation-check-py]
- Maintenance signals are genuinely positive: real CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs a Node/Python job (tests, `npm pack --dry-run`, a plugin-mirror-sync check, attribution scan, secret scan, published score-snapshot) plus a SEPARATE, fully parallel PowerShell job re-implementing the same gates (`Test-Attribution.ps1`, `Test-Secret.ps1`) — maintaining two language implementations of the same test gates is a real cross-platform commitment. `CONTRIBUTING.md` requires "one behavior change per pull request," "one benchmark case for each new failure mode," explicit bans on "no detector-immunity claims" and "no copied competitor wording or examples," and issue templates (`false-positive.md`, `bad-rewrite.md`) map directly onto the benchmark's own failure taxonomy. [slopkit-clone]

## SYNTHESIS

The honest read on slopkit is: it is a real artifact with real engineering discipline in places, and the self-criticism the owner was impressed by is genuine and code-verified — but it is local to one file (`BENCHMARKS.md`) and one script (`competitor_output_score.py`), not a repo-wide property. The sibling benchmark for the other skill (`slopgent`) has real blinding but a hardcoded win-required gate, which is the same category of self-serving benchmark design that `BENCHMARKS.md` criticizes elsewhere in the same repo. This is worth being blunt about rather than treating the one honest file as representative of the whole project's evaluation culture.

**What is worth stealing, ranked by value:**

1. **The `must_flag`-with-required-reason decoy-rejection pattern** (`decoy_rejection.py`'s `FLAG_MAP` design: assert BOTH that the specific detector fires AND that the specific sub-score it targets is measurably depressed, not just that an aggregate score drops). This is the single highest-value thing to adopt, and it's exactly what `judge.mjs` lacks — its own `--self-test` harness (16 recurrence/vocab cases + 8 abstract-noun cases) proves the checker classifies its OWN worked examples correctly, which is a confusion matrix against synthetic fixtures the author wrote to match the checker's own rules, not an adversarial test against a report engineered to look clean while being substantively false. `judge.mjs`'s own header comment already admits this: "A clean --self-test run proves the checker gets those 14 right; it does NOT prove a general low false-positive rate on arbitrary new copy."

2. **Publishing your own losses as runtime output, not just as prose.** `competitor_output_score.py` prints its circularity caveat every time it runs, not only in a separate markdown file — this means the caveat can't silently go stale relative to the code the way a documentation-only claim can. Any comparative scoring script `judge.mjs` or a sibling tool ever grows should bake the same self-check into its own output.

3. **The specific rule classes slopkit's word lists cover that `judge.mjs` does not**: `deslop_lint.py`'s `STRUCTURE_PATTERNS.not_just_but` regex is a working negative-parallelism detector distinct from `judge.mjs`'s own (which already exists — see CHECK 3's `negative-definition` and `rather-than` greps — so this is convergent design, not a gap); slopkit's `triad_count` tricolon regex is comparable to `judge.mjs`'s own tricolon grep (both already flagged as high-false-positive by their respective authors); `orwell_lint.py`'s passive-voice detector with an irregular-participle set and an adjectival-participle exclusion list is more carefully built than a naive be-verb+participle regex would be, and is worth stealing verbatim if `judge.mjs` ever adds passive-voice detection.

4. **What NOT to steal**: the "264 independent judge rows" framing as a template for how to describe a benchmark — it's accurate but easy to misread, and a benchmark description should make explicit whether "independent" means blind-across-competing-systems or independent-rubric-angles-on-one-system, because these are very different evidentiary strengths and the phrase alone doesn't disambiguate. Also do not adopt `judge_aggregate.py`'s win-required gate pattern (`PRIMARY = "slopgent"` + margin) — `competitor_output_score.py`'s own parity-not-superiority framing, in the same repository, is the better model.

**Where slopkit is straightforwardly better than what exists in `judge.mjs` today**: it has a working adversarial-decoy test for at least one of its two skills, it has real (if narrowly-scoped) CI enforcement of a fact-preservation constraint via `preservation_check.py`, and it runs that CI on every push, not just on demand. `judge.mjs` currently has neither an adversarial decoy corpus nor a mechanized fact-preservation check — it only self-tests against fixtures the author wrote to match its own rules. The concrete gap to close: build a `judge.mjs` equivalent of `decoys.jsonl` — a small corpus of PDPP site copy that is mechanically clean (no em-dash, no unearned name, no hard-grep hit) but is still wrong in a way Q1-Q5 of `JUDGE-SPEC.md`'s judged rubric would catch (e.g., a passage that averages across reference sites while using zero banned words, or a passage with a genuinely earned-sounding but ultimately unfounded derivation claim) — and assert the judged rubric rejects it for the SPECIFIC named reason (which Q number, which specific evidence), not merely with a lower overall impression. This is the direct structural analog of slopgent's `must_flag` design applied to `JUDGE-SPEC.md`'s Q1-Q5 rather than to comms_lint's honesty/precision/plain sub-scores.

**What slopkit does NOT do that this project's own `judge.mjs` does**: corpus-relative/frequency-based detection (CHECK 5, abstract-noun drift, comparing a candidate word's usage against the real PDPP spec corpus rather than a fixed universal list) has no analog anywhere in slopkit's codebase — confirmed by the repo-wide grep for frequency/corpus-relative/tf-idf terms returning nothing, and independently confirmed absent across the wider landscape survey (see the sibling tooling-landscape corpus entry). This is a genuine point where the smaller, less-benchmarked tool has done something the more heavily-tested one has not attempted.
