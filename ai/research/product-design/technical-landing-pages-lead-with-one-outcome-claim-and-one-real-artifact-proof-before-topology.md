## CLAIMS

- Stripe leads with a financial-infrastructure-that-grows-revenue claim, pairs it with a precise measurable proof in the hero chrome plus scale/service claims, then expands into platform breadth — one precise proof early, then breadth. [stripe]
- Linear leads with "the product development system for teams and agents" and makes the hero a real product surface running (the hero immediately becomes workflow proof) — show the system itself running, not a diagram of it. [linear]
- Vercel leads with "Build and deploy on the AI Cloud," using brand abstraction plus product taxonomy and use-case rails; strong for authority but weaker for protocol proof absent a concrete artifact. [vercel]
- Plaid leads with "turn data into revolutionary financial products," framing trust/control and relationship first with mechanism later — show the relationship and trust story before the plumbing. [plaid]
- Cloudflare leads with a three-verb thesis ("Connect, protect, and build everywhere") backed by scale/service proof — a three-verb platform thesis works when backed by real scale. [cloudflare]
- Val Town leads with "instantly deploy TypeScript web apps, APIs, AI agents" and shows real code/editor chrome, moving from claim to runnable examples immediately — if the system is code-shaped, show code rather than describing it. [valtown]
- 1Password leads with "secure access for every human and AI agent" and governance/control visuals, moving claim → trust/control proof → system details — the closest analog to a trust story: governance first, mechanism second. [onepassword]
- LaunchDarkly leads with "release safely with precise control," selling flag/rollout control before release topology — control and safety before architecture. [launchdarkly]

## SYNTHESIS

Cross-cutting findings for framing a technical/infrastructure landing page:

1. **The top of the page makes one claim and one proof unavoidable** — one sharp promise, one concrete proof object, one immediate transition into breadth or mechanism. Strong sites do not front-load architecture.
2. **Outcome comes before topology** — the repeated arc is (1) what this unlocks, (2) why you should believe it, (3) how it works. Even for mostly-invisible infrastructure, the opening does not begin with hosting model or pipeline topology.
3. **The most transferable proof pattern is "real artifact, not concept art"** — Linear (real product surface), Val Town (real code surface), and 1Password (access-governance surface) all *show the system running* rather than a decorative explainer.
4. **The "how it works" transition usually happens within one viewport** — promise → proof → mechanism moves quickly, not a long marketing layer followed by a separate docs layer.
5. **Machinery later does not mean vagueness now** — these sites defer technical reality until the reader understands why it matters, without hiding it.

For a mostly-invisible system the infra precedents converge: sell the outcome first, make the invisible visible through one credible control surface, and defer deployment/integration details. The guiding rule across all eight sites: outcome first, believable artifact second, machinery third.
