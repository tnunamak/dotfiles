# Quality writing for general technical documents

Use this reference for plain technical writing. Formal ASD-STE100 is a controlled language
with a specific dictionary. Do not call general technical prose STE-compliant.

## Zinsser's four qualities

William Zinsser named four "articles of faith": clarity, simplicity, brevity, and humanity.
Use them as coupled review questions, not independent quotas.

- **Clarity:** Can the intended reader recover the exact meaning on the first read? Put
  information in task order. Name actors, actions, conditions, and consequences directly.
- **Simplicity:** Use the least complicated language that preserves technical meaning.
  Remove pretension and needless abstraction. Keep and explain necessary domain terms.
- **Brevity:** Cut throat-clearing, repetition, redundant modifiers, and phrases that add
  no meaning. Keep required conditions, exceptions, rationales, and warnings.
- **Humanity:** Sound like one person helping another. Use a natural voice suitable for the
  genre. Do not invent experience, add fake warmth, or make safety material chatty.

When these qualities conflict, preserve correctness and reader safety first. Then prefer
clarity, followed by simplicity and brevity. Add humanity within the document's purpose and
house style.

## Plain technical-writing checklist

- Write for the reader's task, not the source system's internal structure.
- Use short sentences and one idea per sentence when that improves comprehension.
- Put a condition before the action that depends on it.
- Use active voice and imperative verbs for instructions.
- Use one term for one concept. Do not vary names only for style.
- Preserve exact code, command, API, configuration, and UI strings.
- Explain necessary specialist terms on first use.
- Remove filler and hedging only when the source supports a direct claim.
